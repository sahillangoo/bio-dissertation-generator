\chapter{Literature Review: Systematic Theory, Molecular Delimitation, and Dendrobatid Evolution}
\label{chap:lit_review}

\section{Epistemological Foundations of Systematic Zoology}
\label{sec:lit_epistemology}

Systematic zoology rests on the dual imperatives of cataloging Earth's organisms and deciphering the genealogical relationships connecting all life forms \parencite{linnaeus1758,darwin1859}. For more than two centuries following the publication of \taxa{Systema Naturae}, classification was grounded fundamentally in morphological similarity and typological character evaluation. The modern phylogenetic synthesis emerged in the mid-twentieth century with the formalization of cladistic theory by Willi \textcite{hennig1966}. Hennig articulated the foundational principle that only shared derived character states (synapomorphies) provide legitimate evidence of phylogenetic relationship, distinguishing them from ancestral states (symplesiomorphies) and non-homologous similarities (homoplasies).

The advent of statistical phylogenetics in the late twentieth century transformed cladistics into a quantitative, probabilistic discipline. Groundbreaking work by \textcite{felsenstein1985} introduced non-parametric bootstrapping as an objective statistical measure of phylogenetic branch support, establishing a rigorous framework to quantify topological certainty. Concurrently, the development of maximum likelihood and Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms \parencite{ronquist2003mrbayes,nguyen2015iqtree} permitted evolutionary biologists to account for complex substitution rate heterogeneities across molecular sequence alignments.

\section{Species Concepts and the Coalescent Paradigm}
\label{sec:lit_species_concepts}

Disputes surrounding the biological definition of ``species'' have historically constituted one of the most contentious arenas in evolutionary biology. The Biological Species Concept (\textsc{bsc}), championed by Ernst \textcite{mayr1942}, defined species as groups of interbreeding natural populations that are reproductively isolated from other such groups. While conceptually powerful, the operational application of the \textsc{bsc} falters in allopatric populations, hybrid zones, and asexual taxa.

Modern systematic theory has largely converged on the General Lineage Concept (\textsc{glc}) of species, which posits that a species is fundamentally a separately evolving metapopulation lineage. Under this unified paradigm, traditional criteria---such as reciprocal monophyly, reproductive isolation, phenotypic diagnosability, and ecological niche divergence---are viewed as contingent properties acquired during the temporal trajectory of lineage divergence, rather than mandatory operational thresholds.

To objectively operationalize the \textsc{glc}, coalescent theory links population genetics with phylogenetic branching processes. In a panmictic ancestral population of effective size $N_e$, the probability density function for the time $t$ to the most recent common ancestor (\textsc{tmrca}) of two randomly sampled alleles follows an exponential distribution:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coalescent_density}
f(t) = \frac{1}{2N_e} \exp\left( -\frac{t}{2N_e} \right)
\end{equation}

Multilocus coalescent models, such as the multispecies coalescent implemented in \textsc{bpp} \parencite{rannala2003bpp}, evaluate whether discordance among independent gene trees is attributable to deep coalescent events (incomplete lineage sorting, \textsc{ils}) or reflects true lineage divergence. Tree-based heuristic algorithms, including the Poisson Tree Processes (\textsc{ptp}) model \parencite{zhang2013ptp}, differentiate between intraspecific branching events governed by coalescent processes and interspecific branching governed by speciation dynamics.

\section{Molecular Markers in Herpetological Systematics}
\label{sec:lit_markers}

In amphibian systematics, mitochondrial markers---specifically Cytochrome $c$ Oxidase Subunit I (\gene{COI}) and the large ribosomal subunit (\gene{16S rRNA})---have served as foundational tools for lineage discovery and DNA barcoding \parencite{folmer1994primers,hebert2003barcoding}. Due to maternal inheritance, lack of recombination, and an elevated evolutionary rate relative to nuclear exons, mitochondrial DNA (\textsc{mtdna}) exhibits rapid coalescence, facilitating the detection of recent divergence events.

The standardized amplification of the 658-bp \gene{COI} ``Folmer region'' has catalyzed worldwide barcoding initiatives \parencite{hebert2003barcoding}. Sequence divergence between putative taxa is frequently estimated using the Kimura 2-parameter (\textsc{k2p}) model, which accounts for differing rates between transitional substitutions ($P$) and transversional substitutions ($Q$):

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:k2p_distance}
K = -\frac{1}{2} \ln(1 - 2P - Q) - \frac{1}{4} \ln(1 - 2Q)
\end{equation}

However, relying exclusively on mitochondrial loci introduces significant hazards, including susceptibility to pseudogenes (nuclear mitochondrial DNA, \textsc{numts}), adaptive introgression, and matrilineal capture. Consequently, modern herpetological systematic revisions mandate the concatenation or coalescent co-estimation of unlinked nuclear loci, such as the recombination-activating gene 1 (\gene{RAG1}) exon.

\section{Systematic History of the Family Dendrobatidae}
\label{sec:lit_dendrobatidae}

The systematic arrangement of Neotropical poison frogs has undergone repeated revision over the past two centuries. Historically assembled within a single expansive genus, \taxa{Dendrobates} Wagler, 1830, early taxonomists classified species primarily on superficial dorsal chromatic patterns and digital disc expansions. Subsequent anatomical and osteological investigations by Charles Myers and colleagues identified significant variations in tarsal keels, scutes, and mandibular osteology.

With the advent of molecular phylogenetics, \textcite{santos2009amazonian} demonstrated that dendrobatid radiation was tightly coupled with late Miocene Andean orogeny and hydrological rearrangements across the Pebas wetland system. In a monumental monograph, \textcite{brown2011ranitomeya} partitioned the polyphyletic genus \taxa{Ranitomeya} into two monophyletic clades, erecting the genus \taxa{Andinobates} for the high-elevation Andean taxa and restricting \taxa{Ranitomeya} sensu stricto to the lowland and premontane Amazonian clade characterized by cephalic reticulations and dorsolateral striping.

\section{Nomenclatural Governance: The ICZN Framework}
\label{sec:lit_iczn}

Any taxonomic revision culminating in the description of new zoological taxa or the redefinition of species boundaries must strictly comply with the mandatory provisions of the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature \parencite{iczn1999code}. Key statutory pillars include:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The Principle of Binominal Nomenclature (Art.~5):} Every species name consists of a binomen comprising a generic name and a specific epithet.
  \item \textbf{The Principle of Typification (Art.~61):} Each nominal taxon must have a name-bearing type (holotype, lectotype, or neotype) that anchors the objective application of the name.
  \item \textbf{The Principle of Priority (Art.~23):} The valid name of a taxon is the oldest available name applied to it, ensuring nomenclatural stability.
  \item \textbf{Explicit Character Diagnosis (Art.~13):} Every newly proposed nominal taxon published after 1999 must be accompanied by an explicit phenotypic diagnosis differentiating it from all congeners.
\end{enumerate}

By harmonizing rigorous coalescent species delimitation with strict ICZN adherence, modern integrative taxonomy achieves both biological veracity and nomenclatural permanence.
