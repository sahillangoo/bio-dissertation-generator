\section{Phylogenetic Reconstruction and Lineage Discovery}
\label{sec:results_phylogeny}

The concatenated molecular alignment comprised \SI{2028}{bp} across the mitochondrial \gene{COI} (\SI{658}{bp}), mitochondrial \gene{16S rRNA} (\SI{550}{bp}), and nuclear \gene{RAG1} (\SI{820}{bp}) loci for 64 ingroup individuals of \taxa{Ranitomeya} and four outgroup taxa (\taxa{Dendrobates tinctorius}, \taxa{Adelphobates galactonotus}, \taxa{Oophaga pumilio}, and \taxa{Phyllobates terribilis}). Maximum Likelihood and Bayesian analyses yielded congruent, highly supported topologies (\cref{fig:phylo_tree}).

The genus \taxa{Ranitomeya} was recovered as monophyletic with maximal support (\textsc{ufboot} = 100\%, Bayesian posterior probability $\textsc{pp} = 1.0$). Within the \taxa{Ranitomeya fantastica} species group, specimens previously assigned to enigmatic high-elevation populations in the Cordillera Azul formed a deeply divergent, reciprocally monophyletic clade sister to \taxa{Ranitomeya fantastica} sensu stricto (\textsc{ufboot} = 100\%, $\textsc{pp} = 1.0$).

\section{Molecular Divergence and Species Delimitation}
\label{sec:results_delimitation}

Uncorrected pairwise genetic distances ($p$-distances) and Kimura 2-parameter (\textsc{k2p}) distances across the \gene{COI} barcoding locus between the Cordillera Azul population and closely allied taxa are summarized in \cref{tab:genetic_distances}. Divergence between this novel clade and \taxa{Ranitomeya fantastica} averaged \SI{6.4 \pm 0.3}{\percent} in \gene{COI} and \SI{3.1 \pm 0.2}{\percent} in \gene{16S rRNA}, well exceeding the typical species-level thresholds documented in Dendrobatidae \parencite{brown2011ranitomeya,santos2009amazonian}.

Both objective species delimitation frameworks unequivocally validated the independent species status of the Cordillera Azul lineage:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{mPTP Analysis:} Multi-rate Poisson Tree Processes recovered the Cordillera Azul clade as an independent evolutionary unit with an empirical support value of $0.98$.
  \item \textbf{BPP Delimitation:} Coalescent speciation probabilities under the unguided species delimitation algorithm (A10) yielded a posterior probability of speciation $P(\text{speciation}) = 1.00$ across all guide topologies and prior specifications.
\end{itemize}

\section{Morphological and Morphometric Differentiation}
\label{sec:results_morphometrics}

Univariate analysis of adult morphometric measurements demonstrated that the Cordillera Azul population is significantly smaller than \taxa{Ranitomeya fantastica} and possesses distinct body proportions (\cref{tab:morphometrics}). \section{Systematic Account: Formal Taxonomic Description}
\label{sec:results_taxonomy}

\subsection*{Diagnosis}
\spnov{\taxa{Ranitomeya aurata}} is diagnosed by the following combination of characters: (1) small adult size (mean \textsc{svl} \SI{16.42}{\milli\metre} in males, \SI{16.85}{\milli\metre} in females); (2) dorsum jet black with a distinctive, brilliant metallic golden-yellow cephalic hood extending posteriorly as fine dorsolateral stripes; (3) flank and limb ground color deep cobalt blue overlaid by coarse black circular reticulations; (4) gular and venter bright canary yellow with bold black ocelli; (5) teeth absent from maxillae and premaxillae; (6) Finger I distinctly shorter than Finger II; (7) digital discs moderately expanded; (8) distinct pairwise \gene{COI} sequence divergence ($>6.4\%$) and diagnostic substitutions at positions 112 (C$\to$T) and 345 (A$\to$G) of the Folmer alignment.

\subsection*{Differential Diagnosis}
\spnov{\taxa{Ranitomeya aurata}} differs readily from its sister species \taxa{Ranitomeya fantastica} by its significantly smaller adult body size (\textsc{svl} \SI{15.60}--\SI{17.20}{\milli\metre} vs. \SI{17.80}--\SI{20.40}{\milli\metre} in \taxa{R. fantastica}; $P < 0.001$, ANOVA, \cref{app:stats}), its gleaming metallic golden cephalic hood (versus red, orange, or uniform yellow in \taxa{R. fantastica}), and deep cobalt limb reticulations (versus charcoal or light turquoise). It is distinguished from \taxa{Ranitomeya summersi} by possessing dorsal striping and vibrant blue ventral patterning (lacking in \taxa{R. summersi}).

\subsection*{Description of Holotype}
Adult male in excellent preservation state (\textsc{svl} \SI{16.52}{\milli\metre}). Head narrower than body; head width \SI{5.18}{\milli\metre}; head length \SI{4.90}{\milli\metre}. Snout rounded in dorsal view, slightly protruding in lateral profile. Canthus rostralis rounded; loreal region concave. Nostrils directed posterolaterally. Eyes large and prominent, diameter \SI{2.20}{\milli\metre}. Tympanum distinct, circular, diameter \SI{1.10}{\milli\metre}, approximately 50\% of eye diameter. Forelimbs slender; relative lengths of adpressed fingers: $\text{I} < \text{II} < \text{IV} < \text{III}$. Hindlimbs moderately robust; tibia length \SI{7.45}{\milli\metre}; foot length \SI{6.90}{\milli\metre}. Skin of dorsum, flanks, and venter smooth.

\subsection*{Etymology}
The specific epithet \textit{aurata} is a Latin adjective meaning ``golden'' or ``gilded,'' directly referencing the radiant, metallic golden cephalic hood and dorsolateral stripes that distinguish this species in life.
