\noindent
\textbf{Title:} Systematics, Phylogenomics, and Cryptic Diversification of Neotropical Poison Frogs (Amphibia: Dendrobatidae)\\
\textbf{Author:} Sahil Langoo\\
\textbf{Degree:} Doctor of Philosophy\\
\textbf{Institution:} Department of Ecology, Evolution, and Organismal Biology\\
\textbf{Advisor:} Dr. Eleanor Vance, Ph.D.

\vspace{1.5em}

\noindent
The Neotropical realm harbors unparalleled amphibian biodiversity, yet many evolutionary lineages remain obscured by morphological crypsis and phenotypic plasticity. Poison frogs of the family \taxa{Dendrobatidae} represent an exceptional model system for studying diversification, aposematic signaling, chemical defense, and speciation across complex biogeographical landscapes. This dissertation investigates the evolutionary systematics, historical biogeography, and species boundaries of the genus \taxa{Ranitomeya} across Amazonian and Andean transition zones.

By synthesizing multi-locus Sanger sequencing (mitochondrial \gene{COI}, \gene{16S rRNA}, and nuclear \gene{RAG1}) with morphological morphometrics and bioacoustic analysis, this work addresses longstanding systematic ambiguities within the \taxa{Ranitomeya fantastica} species complex. Molecular phylogenetic analyses conducted via Maximum Likelihood and Bayesian Inference reveal pronounced, well-supported topological clades that correspond to discrete hydrological drainage basins and elevation gradients. Multi-rate Poisson Tree Processes (mPTP) and Bayesian species delimitation algorithms (BPP) identify a distinct, previously unrecognized lineage inhabiting the upper Huallaga River valley of northeastern Peru.

Morphometric analysis of 124 museum vouchers corroborates molecular divergence, revealing statistically significant divergence in snout-vent length, head width, and limb proportions. In accordance with the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature (ICZN), this cryptic taxon is formally diagnosed and described as \spnov{Ranitomeya aurata}. Furthermore, historical demographic modeling suggests that Pleistocene climatic oscillations and late Neogene Andean orogenic activity acted in concert to drive allopatric divergence across riverine barriers.

Finally, conservation status evaluations indicate that habitat fragmentation and illegal commercial trafficking threaten localized micro-endemic populations. This dissertation demonstrates the indispensable utility of integrative taxonomy, modern coalescent species delimitation, and museum voucher stewardship in documenting and preserving endangered biodiversity in threatened tropical ecosystems.
