\begin{center}
{\Large \bfseries ABSTRACT\par}
\vspace{0.25in}
\end{center}

\noindent Antimicrobial resistance among Gram-negative bacteria remains a serious clinical problem. Plant extracts continue to be screened because they contain mixed classes of secondary metabolites rather than a single synthetic target.

\vspace{0.12in}

\noindent \textit{Dipsacus inermis} Wall.\ ex DC.\ (Himalayan teasel; Caprifoliaceae) occurs in the Kashmir Himalaya. Most published antibacterial work on this species has used organic-solvent extracts. An aqueous extract, the preparation closest to a traditional decoction, had been less fully described against common Gram-negative laboratory pathogens, including \textit{Klebsiella pneumoniae}.

\vspace{0.12in}

\noindent Whole plants were obtained from the Department of Botany, University of Kashmir, identified, washed, shade-dried, and powdered. An aqueous extract was prepared and filtered. Clinical pathogenic stocks of \textit{Escherichia coli}, \textit{Pseudomonas aeruginosa}, and \textit{Klebsiella pneumoniae} were obtained from the Advanced Research Laboratory, Department of Zoology, University of Kashmir, and tested by disc diffusion. Distilled water served as the negative control. A commercial gentamicin disc served as the positive control.

\vspace{0.12in}

\noindent Qualitative tests showed that the aqueous extract contained the major secondary-metabolite classes screened. Clear zones of inhibition were recorded against all three organisms. The response was concentration-dependent for \textit{E.\ coli} and \textit{K.\ pneumoniae}. \textit{P.\ aeruginosa} was also inhibited, though the higher load did not produce a larger zone than the lower load. Gentamicin produced marked inhibition on every plate. Distilled-water discs produced no clearance. The study is limited to in vitro zone diameters of a crude aqueous extract; it does not establish clinical efficacy or a minimum inhibitory concentration.

\vspace{0.2in}

\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} \textit{Dipsacus inermis}, aqueous extract, disc diffusion, zone of inhibition, \textit{Escherichia coli}, \textit{Pseudomonas aeruginosa}, \textit{Klebsiella pneumoniae}.
