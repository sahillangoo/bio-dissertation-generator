# Chapter Drafting Guidelines for Life Sciences Dissertations

This guide details narrative pacing, academic rigor, and biological argumentation required for Ph.D. and M.S. dissertations in Biology and Zoology.

---

## 1. Chapter 1: Introduction and Conceptual Framework

- **Goal**: Set the intellectual stage. Transition from broad macro-evolutionary or ecological theory to the specific micro-system being investigated.
- **Narrative Arc**:
  1. *The Broad Phenomenon*: What general evolutionary problem is being addressed? (e.g. adaptive divergence across environmental gradients).
  2. *Theoretical Contradictions*: Why does this problem remain contentious? (e.g. balance between divergent natural selection and gene flow).
  3. *The Study Clade*: Why are these organisms ideally suited to address the question? (e.g. *Sceloporus* squamates with well-characterized ecophysiological limits).
  4. *Explicit Hypotheses*: Formulate testable, mutually exclusive hypotheses ($H_1, H_2, H_3$) and clear predictions.

---

## 2. Chapter 2: Literature Review

- **Goal**: Establish deep historical mastery and synthesize competing paradigms.
- **Style Rules**:
  - Avoid a mere "laundry list" of author-date summaries ("Smith found X, Jones found Y").
  - Organize thematically around concepts (e.g., "The Role of Pluvial Lake Isolation in Western Vicariance").
  - Use comparative synthesis tables to summarize sample sizes, molecular markers, and conclusions across previous monographs.

---

## 3. Chapter 3: Materials and Methods

- **Goal**: Uncompromising reproducibility.
- **Components**:
  - Exact geographic localities with decimal degrees (Datum: WGS84) and elevations.
  - Vouchers: Natural history museum acronyms and catalog numbers.
  - Molecular details: Kit extraction specifics, primer sequences (5' to 3'), master mix volumes, thermocycling protocols.
  - Computational pipelines: Software versions (e.g. IQ-TREE v2.2.0), model selection criteria (BIC/AICc), MCMC convergence diagnostics (ESS > 200, PSRF < 1.05).
  - Ethics: Explicit IACUC approval numbers and wildlife collection permits.

---

## 4. Chapter 4: Results

- **Goal**: Impartial empirical presentation without speculative interpretation.
- **Golden Rule**: Keep results separate from discussion. Describe patterns observed in data, test statistics, and significance levels.
- **Reporting Hierarchy**:
  - Sequence alignment and summary statistics (number of parsimony-informative sites).
  - Phylogenetic tree topology and branch support (bootstrap support / posterior probability).
  - Morphometric distributions, PCA loadings, and statistical hypothesis tests.

---

## 5. Chapter 5: Discussion

- **Goal**: Biological synthesis and broader scientific impact.
- **Framework**:
  1. *Direct Hypothesis Testing*: Did results support or refute $H_1$, $H_2$, and $H_3$?
  2. *Mechanistic Interpretation*: What ecological or selective forces explain these patterns?
  3. *Concordance with Prior Studies*: How do these findings reshape conclusions from earlier authors?
  4. *Limitations & Caveats*: Honest appraisal of marker resolution, sampling gaps, or statistical power.
  5. *Future Trajectories*: Concrete recommendations for next-generation research.
