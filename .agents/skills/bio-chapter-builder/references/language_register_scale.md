# Language Register Scale for Scientific Dissertations

This rubric turns the Clinical Empiricist Protocol into a language test. That protocol is Band 5 (high complexity). It is not the writing target for this M.Sc. Zoology dissertation.

**Target for chapters in this dissertation: Band 2–3 (clear scientific English).** Register hygiene must always pass.

Do not impose decimal indexing (`1.0`, `1.1.1`) on the thesis body. Keep the existing LaTeX `\chapter` / `\section` structure.

---

## 1. What the protocol contributes

Keep from the protocol:

- Third person in chapters (no authorial `I` / `we` / `you`).
- No colloquialisms, idioms, slang, or conversational filler.
- No metaphors, analogies, rhetorical questions, or persuasive hype.
- Cautious verbs when a claim goes beyond the measured data (`indicates`, `suggests`, `was not measured`).

Drop from the protocol:

- Mandatory all-passive voice.
- Latinate padding used only for exactitude of tone.
- Compound-complex sentences built to stack several conditions in one clause.

---

## 2. Five scoring dimensions (1–5)

Score each chapter on the four numeric dimensions. Register hygiene is pass/fail.

### 2.1 Voice

| Band | Description |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 | First or second person in scientific chapters (`I found`, `we measured`, `you can see`). |
| 2 | Mostly third person; occasional mild personification of the study (`this work shows`). |
| 3 | Third person throughout. Mix of active and passive (`This study measured…`; `Zones were recorded…`). **Target.** |
| 4 | Almost all passive; agents disappear (`It was ascertained that…`). |
| 5 | Rigid third-person passive with no human or study agent (`It was subsequently determined that…`). |

### 2.2 Lexicon

| Band | Description |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 | Everyday wording that drops necessary scientific terms. |
| 2 | Plain verbs and short nouns; technical terms kept only when needed (Soxhlet, LPS, MIC). **Target low end.** |
| 3 | Standard scientific vocabulary without ornamental Latinate diction. **Target high end.** |
| 4 | Frequent padding (`utilize`, `aforementioned`, `subsequently`, `facilitate`). |
| 5 | Polysyllabic, Latin-derived wording chosen for formality (`paramount`, `seminal`, `daunting`, `refractory structural envelope`). |

Necessary terms to keep: Soxhlet, agar-well diffusion, LPS, RND, MIC, iridoid, saponin, binomial names.

Padding to drop or replace: utilize → use; subsequent to → after; seminal / profound / daunting / fascinating / renowned → delete or a plain word.

### 2.3 Syntax

| Band | Description |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 | Fragments or telegraphic notes. |
| 2 | Short sentences; one main idea each. **Target.** |
| 3 | Some coordination; still easy to parse. **Acceptable.** |
| 4 | Long sentences with several clauses. |
| 5 | Stacked compound-complex sentences that isolate multiple conditions at once. |

### 2.4 Epistemic caution

| Band | Description |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 | Overclaiming (clinical efficacy, “first report”, MIC from zone diameter). |
| 2 | Mostly accurate; a few unhedged literature generalisations. |
| 3 | Hedges match the evidence (`indicates`, `suggests`, `was not measured`). **Target.** |
| 4 | Appropriate caution, slightly wordy. **Acceptable.** |
| 5 | Hedge-stuffed prose that hides the finding (`It may perhaps be tentatively suggested that…`). |

### 2.5 Register hygiene (pass / fail)

Fail if any of the following appear in scientific chapters:

- Colloquialisms, idioms, or conversational filler.
- Rhetorical questions, analogies, or metaphors.
- Emotional or promotional wording.
- Emojis or exclamation points.

Acknowledgements may use first person and thanks. That genre is scored separately and is not required to match chapter voice.

---

## 3. Band labels

| Overall band | Label | Use in this dissertation |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| 1 | Informal or under-specified | No |
| 2 | Clear scientific English | Yes |
| 3 | Standard scientific English | Yes |
| 4 | Dense academic English | Rewrite toward 2–3 |
| 5 | Clinical empiricist English | Rewrite toward 2–3 |

Overall band is the rounded mean of Voice, Lexicon, Syntax, and Epistemic caution, provided hygiene is pass. Hygiene fail keeps the chapter out of target until cleaned.

---

## 4. Worked examples (same facts)

Band 5: “Gram-negative bacteria represent the most daunting challenge in antimicrobial pharmacology due to their uniquely refractory structural envelope.”

Band 2–3: “Gram-negative bacteria are difficult to inhibit because of their outer membrane.”

Band 5: “…displaying a fascinating flowering dynamic wherein florets open in an expanding annular zone originating in the center of the head…”

Band 2–3: “Florets open in a ring that spreads from the centre of the head toward the base and the tip.”

---

## 5. Locked rewrite constraints

- Do not change numbers, tables, figure files, citations, or binomials.
- Do not loosen claim limits already in the text (no MIC, no clinical efficacy, indexed gap is not a “first report”).
- Prefer “This study measured…” / “The extract produced…” over “It was subsequently ascertained that…”
- Do not rewrite institutional front matter (title, certificate, declaration).
