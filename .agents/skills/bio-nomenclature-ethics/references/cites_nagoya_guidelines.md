# CITES & Nagoya Protocol International Compliance Guide

This guide outlines ethical and regulatory requirements for research involving foreign biological specimens, tissue transfers, and genetic resources.

---

## 1. Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species (CITES)

Whenever biological specimens, tissue samples, venom, blood, or DNA are imported or exported across international borders:

1. **Check Appendix Status**:
   - **Appendix I**: Species threatened with extinction. Commercial trade is prohibited. Scientific transfer requires **both** an Import Permit from the receiving country and an Export Permit from the origin country.
   - **Appendix II**: Species not currently threatened but which may become so unless trade is strictly regulated. Requires a valid CITES Export Permit (or re-export certificate) issued by the Management Authority of the exporting state.
   - **Appendix III**: Species protected in at least one country that has asked for assistance. Requires an export permit from the listing state or certificate of origin.
2. **Dissertation Reporting**:
   - Record permit numbers in the Methods section and in the specimen voucher appendix:
     ```latex
     International tissue transport was authorized under CITES Export Permit 
     \textbf{US-24-91823} and USFWS Import Declaration Form 3-177.
     ```

## 2. Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit-Sharing (ABS)

The Nagoya Protocol governs access to genetic resources and fair sharing of benefits arising from their utilization.

### Required Documentation
- **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)**: Official permission granted by the competent national authority of the provider country prior to collecting or exporting genetic resources.
- **Mutually Agreed Terms (MAT)**: Formal agreement specifying the research conditions, benefit sharing (e.g., co-authorship, technology transfer, training), and restrictions on commercialization.
- **Internationally Recognized Certificate of Compliance (IRCC)**: Generated via the ABS Clearing-House (`https://absch.cbd.int/`).

### In-Text Citation Format
```latex
Genetic samples were obtained in compliance with the Nagoya Protocol on Access 
and Benefit Sharing under Internationally Recognized Certificate of Compliance 
\textbf{ABSCH-IRCC-CR-254109-1}.
```

## 3. Museum Voucher Deposition Rules

- Molecular phylogenies must be anchored to verifiable physical specimens.
- "Voucherless" sequences are heavily discouraged by taxonomic journals (*Zootaxa*, *Systematic Biology*).
- All physical vouchers must be permanently deposited in an indexed natural history museum (e.g., MVZ, USNM, MCZ, AMNH, BMNH).
