**Citation**: Smith, M. L., Jezkova, T., & Leaché, A. D. (2021). Phylogenomics and ecological speciation in western phrynosomatid lizards. *Molecular Phylogenetics and Evolution*, 158, 107080. doi:10.1016/j.ympev.2021.107080

## 1. Summary of Study

Smith et al. (2021) investigated genomic divergence and environmental niche modeling across 12 phrynosomatid taxa in western North America using RAD-seq (12,450 loci). Key findings include:
1. **Dating of Diversification**: Major clade splits within the *Sceloporus occidentalis* group date to the Late Pliocene (~2.8–3.2 Ma), coinciding with the uplift of the Sierra Nevada rain shadow.
2. **Gene Flow Across Contact Zones**: Significant nuclear introgression occurs at low-elevation passes (e.g., Walker Pass, San Gorgonio Pass), despite distinct mitochondrial haplogroups.
3. **Environmental Niche Divergence**: E-niche disparity between Sierra and Great Basin lineages exceeds expectations under neutral drift, implicating thermal selection.

## 2. Key Takeaways for My Dissertation

- **Discordance between Mitochondrial and Nuclear Data**:
  - Smith et al. found that mtDNA trees placed certain eastern Sierra populations inside the Great Basin clade, whereas nuclear clustering placed them firmly with Pacific coastal lineages.
  - In my study, I must explicitly test for cytonuclear discordance between my COI and RAG1 topologies.
- **Morphological Gap in Smith et al.**:
  - Smith et al. collected extensive molecular data but did not measure specimen morphology or functional traits (sprint speed, thermal performance).
  - My dissertation bridges this exact gap by pairing molecular phylogenetics with detailed cranial and postcranial morphometrics across the same contact zone.

## 3. Potential Counter-Arguments and Nuances

- Smith et al. used a strict clock prior for RAD loci based on a general squamate substitution rate (1.2% per lineage per Myr). However, fossil-calibrated node dating in RevBayes may yield older crown estimates. I should compare both calibrations in Chapter 4.
