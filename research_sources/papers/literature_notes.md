## 2. Conceptual Themes to Emphasize in Chapter 2

### 2.1 The Pleistocene Vicariance vs. Gradient Speciation Debate
- Early literature (e.g., Stebbins 1954, Savage 1960) emphasized geographic isolation in pluvial lake basins during Pleistocene glacial maxima as the primary engine of speciation in western North American squamates.
- Recent phylogenomic analyses (e.g., Leaché et al. 2017, Smith et al. 2021) suggest ecological divergent selection along steep elevational gradients may promote speciation even in the presence of continuous or secondary gene flow.
- My dissertation tests this by evaluating whether morphological divergence (limb lengths, cranial dimensions) aligns with ecotonal thermal boundaries rather than hydrologic drainage basins.

### 2.2 Ecomorphological Divergence in Rock-Dwelling vs. Arboreal Ectotherms
- Phrynosomatid lizards display well-documented morphological adaptations to substrate use (Losos 1990; Melville & Swain 2000).
- Saxicolous (rock-dwelling) morphotypes typically possess flatter skulls (reduced head depth) and longer hindlimbs to optimize sprint speed on inclined rock surfaces.
- Arboreal populations exhibit shorter limb proportions facilitating stability on narrow branches and twigs.
- Our Great Basin dataset spans both boulder-field talus habitats and pinyon-juniper woodland, offering an ideal micro-evolutionary test.

## 3. Methodological Literature Notes

- **Phylogenetic Inconsistency with Single Loci**: Folmer et al. (1994) COI primers provide excellent resolution for terminal population branching, but deep nodes in *Sceloporus* require slow-evolving nuclear markers like RAG1 (Groth & Barrowclough 1999) to avoid saturation at 3rd codon positions.
- **Model Partitioning**: Lanfear et al. (2012) demonstrated that oversimplified partitioning schemes inflate branch lengths and misestimate divergence times. I will partition by codon position for protein-coding genes (COI positions 1, 2, 3; RAG1 positions 1, 2, 3).
